Property Revaluation Experts Mar 2023 – Present
  • AI-native internal tooling: personally designed and built a library of Claude-powered agent workflows (property valuation review, sales-ratio study analysis, lease review, contractor scope generation, deal underwriting) that run day-to-day service delivery, owning problem definition, guardrail design, evaluation, and iteration end to end.
  • County valuation system: Georgia law lays out a specific sequence a county has to follow to recalibrate property values: verify the input data, diagnose where the ratio is failing, calibrate land first, model the cost and depreciation scenarios, validate each one for compliance, then deliver a board-ready package (O.C.G.A. § 48-5-343). I built the system that runs that sequence. For one county whose residential cost model hadn't been recalibrated in years and whose state-audited sales ratio sat well under the legal floor, I rebuilt the underlying parcel and sales database (6,266 parcels, 23,710 sale records spanning a multi-year sales history), reconciled the cost model's median ratio to within about 1.4% of parity against real sales data, then ran a large scenario sweep that showed the model's shape was right and only its calibration was off. Every scenario gets validated against both Georgia statute and IAAO (International Association of Assessing Officers) dispersion standards before it's eligible for delivery, not just internally reviewed. That let us implement a phased rate correction instead of guessing at a new schedule. The certified digest moved from $621.0M to $760.6M and the equalized sales ratio from 20.59 to 28.58.
  • Sales-ratio audit reconciliation: a separate Claude-driven workflow from the recalibration pipeline above. When that county's sales-ratio study was formally challenged, it pulled the disputed sample set and checked each one against the actual recorded deed instead of the field note that first went into the file, catching sales that had been logged on assumptions the deed didn't support. The state auditor's office reviewed the resulting corrections and put its own position in writing: the study, as revised, is correct.
  • Statistical rigor behind that system: the validation layer is the real gate, not a formality. Every scenario has to clear Georgia's own compliance thresholds (coefficient of dispersion, price-related differential, assessment-ratio band) before it's even eligible to be recommended, and that check runs identically no matter which valuation model produced the scenario, so no method gets to grade its own homework. Every sale is stratified by property class and neighborhood, and the ratio study is diagnosed at the stratum level instead of a single countywide average. A portion of sales is held out and never touched during calibration, so the fit gets checked against data it hasn't seen. Replacement cost starts from national cost tables, then gets corrected against real local sales class by class, with the data support, method, and confidence behind every correction documented rather than applied as one blanket adjustment. Depreciation is fit from what the market actually shows on older properties, not assumed from a published table, and several depreciation methods get run and scored against each other rather than picked in advance. Nothing reaches the county without clearing a coverage threshold first; a partial-data result gets flagged as a pilot or blocked outright, never delivered quietly as final.
  • Adoption-led rollout: ran intake, training, and change management directly with the county's own assessment team, so the review process is something they can repeat without me in the room, not tooling I shipped and walked away from.
  • Outcome ownership: killed or reworked workflows that weren't delivering measured value instead of letting them linger; defined success metrics before build, not after.
